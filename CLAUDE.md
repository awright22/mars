# Mars Survival (working title): project instructions

This repository is a phone-first 3D survival game on a whole, real Mars, played in the browser and installable
as a PWA. The name is a working title until the owner chooses one; it lives in `game/src/core/brand.js`, and the
build writes it into the page and the manifest. `README.md` describes the game as it stands.

Layout: `game/` is the published site (`index.html`, the PWA files, the built `game.js`, `game.css`,
`terrain-worker.js` and `sw.js`, `assets/`, and the source in `game/src/`). `tools/` holds the build
(`build.mjs`), the local server, lint, the test suite (`tools/tests/`), publishing (`publish.mjs`), the Mars data
build and the Blender model scripts (`tools/assets/`). `QUALITY.md` is the bar and the open items; `CREDITS.md`
the third-party credits. The published build also ships `game/licenses.txt`, generated by the build.

## What the game is
These points are the game, and they stay:
1. An original story of survival on a real Mars. The player is Quinn, the one-person advance mission
   Groundwork, sent ahead to switch on Arcadia Station before the first full crew flies. A solar storm wrecks the
   landing far off target and darkens every relay. Quinn survives (air, power, water from ice and baked
   regolith, a farm), builds an Earth-link from the radio parts of real historical landers, holds out while the
   station's propellant plant fills the ascent vehicle, drives to Arcadia Station, files the handover for the
   first crew, launches alone and docks with the empty transit ship Tern to go home. Everything in it is the
   game's own: no names, lines, jokes or distinctive chain of events borrowed from any book, film or game (see
   `QUALITY.md`, "Originality").
2. "Feature rich on par with Stardew Valley or Animal Crossing ... a super complete survival game": real depth
   and breadth of systems and things to do, not a short demo.
3. Real Mars. It is geographically correct from real maps of Mars, the planet is a sphere you can explore
   entirely, and you start at a random place on it.
4. Real 3D assets (built in Blender, or of that quality), a third-person astronaut you can see whole, and
   animations for the things the astronaut does.
5. It plays on a phone as an installable PWA, as well as on a desktop.

The owner's bar: "the goal is now a solid indie release, not AAA. 'Nearly' on the picture and interface bars is
good enough." That means a game that looks, sounds, plays and holds together like a solid independent release,
on a phone and on a desktop. A bar in `QUALITY.md` that a reviewer marks "nearly" counts as reached.

Direction the owner has given, which stays:
- The phone comes first. The iPhone held sideways is the machine the game is made for and judged on. The
  desktop must keep working, and comes second.
- Two modes. Story mode (the story above, ending in the launch) and Base mode ("Stay": the Tern is lost in the
  storm, the next ship is 26 months out, and Quinn builds the place it will land; Valheim-like building, no
  enemies, no rescue).
- The order: Story mode first. Base mode work waits until Story mode meets its bar.

## What is yours
Within the five points above you have full authority over design, art direction, systems, balance, UI, audio,
controls, code structure and tooling. You may rewrite, restructure or redesign anything. Merge duplicated code
when you meet it, and fix what you find on the way.

## Where it runs
- The live game is this repository's `gh-pages` branch, served by GitHub Pages at
  https://awright22.github.io/mars/. `node tools/publish.mjs "what changed"` builds, lints, runs the full
  suite and publishes; only a build whose full suite passes is published. The service-worker cache version is a
  hash of every shipped file, so each build bumps it and installed copies pick it up. Earlier builds stay in the
  branch's history.
- The game also runs as plain static files (`node tools/serve.mjs`) and works offline once loaded.
- The old address (awright22/workos-test-app's gh-pages) now only redirects here, and offers to copy a save
  that is still in an old home-screen copy's storage.

## Constraints that stay
- Assets may be procedural, authored in Blender through `tools/assets/`, or third-party where the licence allows
  redistribution. Credit third-party assets and data in `CREDITS.md`, and keep `game/licenses.txt` complete.
- No agency logo or insignia in any model, texture or icon, and no real astronaut's name or likeness. Real
  agencies appear only as the builders of the historical landers. The credits say the game is not affiliated
  with or endorsed by any agency.
- Photosensitivity: a notice on first launch, and no full-screen or large-area flash more than three times a
  second (`ui/dom.js` flashScreen is rate-limited; large lights vary smoothly, `game/launch.js` flicker).
- Players keep saves in their browsers. When a change breaks the save format, migrate old saves; where that is
  not possible, tell the player plainly on load that the save is from an older version. Saves can be copied out
  and pasted in as text (Settings, Saved games).
- Never open, print or commit `.env`, or any key or secret.

## Approvals
Work without asking on anything within the five points: edits, rewrites, deleting or replacing the game's files,
commits and pushes to the working branch, publishing a build that passes its checks, installing development tools,
and retrying after errors. Ask the owner first only for a change to one of the five points. Keep the platform's
own confirmation step for destructive or irreversible operations.

## Finishing work
Work on what the owner asks for, finish it, publish it, record the state in `QUALITY.md`, then stop until the
owner writes again.

## How to know it is good
Quality is judged from the running game, not from the code.
- Play it. Chromium and Playwright are installed in cloud sessions. Drive the real game in a browser, desktop
  size and phone size with touch, capture screenshots and short sequences, and look at them before you call
  anything better. The debug hook (`window.__MG`) reaches any place, time of day and game state.
- `QUALITY.md` holds a bar for each area and what has been measured against it: frame rate (60 fps on a
  mid-range laptop, 30 fps on a recent iPhone; headless software rendering is far slower than a real GPU, so say
  which you measured), load time, zero console errors, the picture at dawn, noon, dusk and night, in a dust storm
  and at the poles, the first ten minutes of a new player, every screen at phone width under the iPhone's status
  bar and notch, and save and load.
- Your own look at your own picture is a weak check. For a visual or feel change, give a reviewer subagent the
  screenshots and the bar, not your conclusion, and act on what it finds.
- Keep the open items in `QUALITY.md`, each with its state.

## Subagents
Delegate when the work splits into independent parts. Each subagent costs a full context of its own, so do small
things yourself. Brief each one with the goal, the files and the bar.

## Communicating clearly
The owner usually follows from a phone. Put the key point first, use plain words and full sentences, and never
use a name you invented during the work without saying what it is. Attach screenshots when a change is visual.
